MATH FRONTIER SEMINAR

Sample complexity lower bounds for learning solution operators of advective PDEs

Christopher Wang Cornell MATH
Time 4:00 pm, 10/19
Location Sage 2715
Food Pizza

Abstract

Learning solution operators of PDEs from input-output data is an important task in scientific machine learning. While current approaches can be quite successful for diffusion-dominated equations, advection-dominated equations prove much more difficult. I'll show how statistical learning theory allows one to derive sample complexity lower bounds for learning solution operators of certain classes of advective equations from data, using the transport equation as a toy model. At the end, I'll give some results showing that learning the solution operator of some advective equations can be impossible, even with added diffusion, if the input data are too smooth.

(Joint work with Diana Halikias and Alex Townsend.)

About the Speaker

Chris is a PhD candidate at Cornell University being advised by Alex Townsend. His primary research interests are in randomized numerical linear algebra and in PDE operator learning.
